# Kaggle winner evidence for DACON 236749 redesign

Checked: 2026-09-17 KST. Research only; no training, model downloads, paid resources, or submissions were performed. Winner claims below are backed by organizers or Kaggle's placement metadata; technical descriptions come from the winners themselves. Proposed transfers are hypotheses, not demonstrated DACON improvements.

## Current decision context and constraints

The parent audit supplied this working baseline: PANNs presence, HTDemucs stems, frozen DF-Arena1B voice, frozen MERT330 plus a small temporal music head, and `File=max(vp*voice,mp*music)`. It also supplied the official-score comparison and recent head-ablation observations. Those local artifacts are outside this note's ownership and were not independently re-audited here. In particular, the reported failure of raw ensembling and pooled File MLPs is a reason to require new evidence before repeating them.

The live [DACON rules](https://dacon.io/competitions/official/236749/overview/rules) permit qualifying public external resources with documented sources and licenses. They prohibit evaluator-data training, tuning, and pseudo-labeling, and prohibit using other evaluation files' statistics or predictions to alter a file's result. Aggregating segments from the same file is explicitly permitted. All adaptation proposed below happens before submission using allowed training/external data; inference weights and calibration remain fixed.

The live [evaluation specification](https://dacon.io/competitions/official/236749/overview/evaluation) sets one L4 with 22.4 GiB VRAM, 28 GB RAM, 6 CPU cores, a 60-minute execution limit, a 10 GB ZIP limit, and 32 GB after unpacking. Public uses 100% of the evaluation data and equals final Private; this is different from Kaggle private-test shakeups. The [task description](https://dacon.io/competitions/official/236749/overview/description) specifies 1,200 files, 16 kHz, mono/stereo, 4–60 seconds, telephone conditions, and simultaneous/sequential mixtures; singing vocals count as voice. These constraints were also independently checked by the parent audit.

### 1. Freesound 2019: reliability-aware training and validated stacking

The winner used log-mel inputs and a CNN with attention, skip connections, and auxiliary classifiers. Training combined five folds, SpecAugment, MixUp, and a smooth temporal concatenation augmentation. Curated labels used BCE; noisy labels used `Lsoft` with beta 0.7 and different sampling probabilities. The author manually reviewed low-scoring curated examples and used BCE for selected reliable noisy examples. The submitted blend combined seven first-level models and three second-level MLPs using a geometric mean; first-level combinations were selected by CV. These details are in the [winner's SHA-pinned README](https://github.com/lRomul/argus-freesound/blob/4faf8f192035b413e8946bda3555474cb9ad8237/README.md).

**Transfer hypothesis:** distinguish verified component labels from weak whole-file labels, and fit any fusion on predictions from examples unseen by the underlying model. Human review should target uncertain training provenance/labels. **Do not copy:** five random folds when source-song or speaker variants can leak, an unvalidated MLP stack, or categorical audio-tag MixUp targets for mixed authentic/fake components. The historical one-hour GPU kernel rule does not establish present L4 feasibility.

### 2. BirdCLEF 2024: match the domain before enlarging the model

The winners used only 2024 train audio plus pseudo-labeled supplied unlabeled soundscapes. They removed duplicates, filtered/relabelled chunks using Google's bird classifier, and injected teacher labels into train labels with coefficient 0.05. Inputs were 10-second chunks formed from adjacent 5-second chunks with averaged labels. Models were ImageNet-pretrained `efficientnet_b0` and `regnety_008`; training used CE, random segments, XY masks, and horizontal CutMix. They reported that BCE, MixUp, added Xeno-canto data, and mismatched 10-second training/5-second inference hurt; larger or more elaborate alternatives were not reliably better. [Winner's report](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/birdclef-2024/writeups/team-kefir-1st-place-solution).

**Transfer hypothesis:** audit source and mixture distribution, keep train/inference windows compatible, and test modest specialists before another full encoder replacement. **Do not copy:** bird-class CE, the 0.05 teacher coefficient, loudness filtering, or removal of difficult examples without a DACON-specific reason. In particular, its soundscape pseudo-labeling recipe must never be applied to DACON evaluation audio. Weak labels from permitted external/training audio are the only possible analogue.

### 3. DFDC: local evidence, nuisance robustness, and aggregation

The winner classified individual face crops with Noisy-Student-pretrained EfficientNet B7 at 380×380. It used 32 video frames and a confidence-based aggregation rule rather than always averaging. Augmentations included compression, noise, blur, resize variation, and dropping facial regions/artifacts. The documented training script trained five B7 models with different seeds; this is a training count, not a claim about five submitted checkpoints. [Winner's SHA-pinned README](https://github.com/selimsef/dfdc_deepfake_challenge/blob/89c6290490bac96b29193a4061b3db9dd3933e36/README.md).

Meta reports that the eventual winner was fourth on the public set and that other winning systems moved substantially in rank. [Organizer results](https://ai.meta.com/datasets/dfdc/). **Transfer hypothesis:** test chunk-level forensic evidence and aggregation against benign transmission changes. **Do not copy:** face processing, EfficientNet B7 solely because it won, image augmentations, or its numerical aggregation thresholds. Video-frame voting does not establish how briefly present fake music/voice should be pooled. DACON's current Public=Private policy also makes the historical public/private ranking lesson an argument for local robustness, not a forecast of a DACON shakeup.

## One directly relevant music-forgery source

[SONICS, ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14080v4) addresses end-to-end synthetic songs, distinguishing that task from synthetic singing over real accompaniment. It motivates long-range temporal modeling with SpecTTTra. The [official pinned repository](https://github.com/awsaf49/sonics/blob/9156ffad151f797c71556923c4a02fa01fa8fc91/README.md) publishes 5-second and 120-second variants; its reported F1/time/memory are its own benchmark results, not DACON EER or L4 measurements. It also says real songs are supplied as YouTube metadata, not bundled audio.

This supports testing whether temporal forensic information survives the current representation, but it does **not** prove a 120-second model is suitable for 4–60-second evaluation files, that whole-song targets are valid stem labels, or that SONICS is unseen by current training. A candidate should demonstrate complementarity before being added. The [pinned license](https://github.com/awsaf49/sonics/blob/9156ffad151f797c71556923c4a02fa01fa8fc91/LICENSE) states MIT for software, CC BY-NC 4.0 for the dataset, and additional Suno/Udio restrictions/terms. Preserve the full conditions in the resource ledger; do not reduce them to an unrestricted-use claim.

## Six concrete design lessons and falsifiable adoption gates

These are proposed experiments, not actions performed or claimed winner techniques. Numerical thresholds below are suggested decision gates to lock before experimentation; the parent can revise them to reflect validation size and training budget. Report class counts with each stratum: EER requires both authentic and fake examples. Recalculate spectral parameters for 16 kHz input; upsampling to a pretrained model's native sample rate cannot restore evidence above the original 8 kHz Nyquist limit.

| Priority | Concrete change to test | Why it follows from the evidence | Falsifiable adoption criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build source-disjoint, generator-aware validation with known/unknown masks for voice and music authenticity. Group original songs, speakers, edits, stems, mixtures, and near-duplicate encodes before splitting. Keep a fixed mixed/telephone/short-clip diagnostic set. | Freesound explicitly treated label reliability; BirdCLEF explicitly curated target-domain training data. Reliable comparison comes before more capacity. | Reject a candidate-selection split containing a source family on both sides. Do not report a full composite when required labels are missing. Accept a candidate only with a positive gain on a fully labeled locked set, reporting group-bootstrap uncertainty and every official component. |
| 2 | Test temporal authenticity predictions before pooling, first on existing cached features. Compare mean, max, fixed top-k, and learned masked attention on identical windows; include position-shifted short fake spans. Diagnose cases where presence multiplication suppresses a correct fake prediction. | The three cases all preserve or aggregate local evidence, while SONICS supplies a music-specific temporal motivation. None proves the correct pooling rule here. | Require at least 0.01 absolute File-EER reduction over the unchanged incumbent on the locked mixed set, with no material fully labeled composite regression. A gain that disappears when the same fake span moves from beginning to end fails. Measure all-real false positives across clip lengths to catch max-pooling multiplicity effects. |
| 3 | Train controlled simultaneous/sequential mixtures and telephone/channel variants from allowed, labeled sources. Track which component is fake and when it is audible. Derive File, Voice, Music, and presence targets from the construction and verified semantics. | Freesound and BirdCLEF used different successful augmentations, and DFDC emphasized nuisance robustness. Their disagreement means augmentation must match this target, not a generic recipe. | At fixed sources, seeds, and updates, require lower weighted error on held-out real/fake component combinations and telephone conditions. Reject if gains depend on codec identifying the label, or if added fake components are assigned an averaged weak File target without justification. Keep a clean-condition check to detect destroyed forensic cues. |
| 4 | Add one small forensic branch with a different representation or forensic pretraining only after diagnosing a residual error class. One concrete candidate is a short-window log-mel CNN trained on labeled mixtures, compared alone and as a fixed-weight complement to MERT330. Use out-of-fold predictions to fit any calibrator or stack. | Freesound's second-level model followed CV selection; BirdCLEF demonstrates that compact backbones can suffice. These do not justify stacking correlated errors. | Accept only if the branch reduces held-out composite error beyond the same incumbent plus seed variation and improves the diagnosed residual class. A weaker single model is admissible only with repeatable ensemble gain. Do not repeat the reported failed raw blend/pooled MLP merely under a new name. |
| 5 | Audit existing presence gating and file fusion with fully labeled mixtures. Compare the existing max-product with a small fixed fusion fitted only on training/validation predictions. Keep conditional Voice/Music scores distinct from presence. | Local-evidence aggregation is transferable from DFDC; the actual formula must reflect DACON's component semantics. | Require improved File EER without damaging the conditional component metrics or the full composite. Pure monotonic calibration of one isolated score normally preserves rank-based EER; justify calibration by its effect on cross-stream fusion, not as automatic standalone EER improvement. Reject any inference rule that needs other evaluation files. |
| 6 | Spend inference budget on the smallest accepted complement. If an expensive offline teacher helps, test distillation into a compact temporal student using permitted training audio, and benchmark the packaged end-to-end pipeline. | BirdCLEF favored compact architectures; Freesound had a bounded inference environment. Distillation here is a proposed engineering route, not a claim about those three winners. | Target at most 50 minutes locally on the specified L4 with 10 minutes of margin, including decode, separation, resampling, inference, and output. Verify numerical parity after any precision/export change. A full pipeline that exceeds memory, archive, or execution limits fails regardless of local EER. |

### Runtime interpretation

The official 60-minute limit over 1,200 files allows **3 seconds per file on average including all overhead**; a proposed 50-minute target allows **2.5 seconds**. These are arithmetic budget bounds, not throughput measurements. All-60-second inputs would contain 20 hours of audio, making the hard bound an end-to-end real-time factor of 0.05; the actual duration distribution is not assumed to be all-60-second. Existing separation and encoders already consume part of that budget. Do not infer that a small head is cheap overall if it introduces another complete backbone or stem pass.

## Decision for the redesign

The strongest transferable direction is **better supervised mixtures and temporal forensic evidence, selected under the full metric**, followed by one complementary, measured branch if necessary. These sources do not identify a universally superior replacement backbone or support a promised first-place score. They provide concrete hypotheses and rejection criteria. Given the parent-reported failed full-finetuning and raw/fusion ablations, a larger architecture or an ensemble is not itself evidence of progress.
